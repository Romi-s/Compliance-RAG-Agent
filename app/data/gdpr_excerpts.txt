GENERAL DATA PROTECTION REGULATION (GDPR) — REGULATION (EU) 2016/679
Curated excerpts for demonstration purposes. Source text is public EU legislation.

==================================================================
DEFINITIONS (Article 4)
==================================================================
'Personal data' means any information relating to an identified or identifiable
natural person ('data subject'). 'Processing' means any operation performed on
personal data, such as collection, recording, storage, use, disclosure, or erasure.
'Controller' means the entity that determines the purposes and means of processing.
'Processor' means an entity that processes personal data on behalf of the controller.

==================================================================
RIGHTS OF THE DATA SUBJECT (Chapter III, Articles 12–23)
==================================================================
GDPR grants data subjects a set of enforceable rights over their personal data:

- Right to information and transparency (Articles 13–14): individuals must be told
  who is processing their data, why, on what legal basis, and for how long.
- Right of access (Article 15): the data subject has the right to obtain confirmation
  of whether their personal data is being processed and, if so, a copy of that data.
- Right to rectification (Article 16): the right to have inaccurate personal data
  corrected and incomplete data completed.
- Right to erasure, the 'right to be forgotten' (Article 17): the right to have
  personal data deleted in certain circumstances.
- Right to restriction of processing (Article 18): the right to limit how data is used.
- Right to data portability (Article 20): the right to receive personal data in a
  structured, commonly used, machine-readable format and to transmit it to another controller.
- Right to object (Article 21): the right to object to processing, including for
  direct marketing, at any time.
- Rights related to automated decision-making and profiling (Article 22): the right
  not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that produces
  legal or similarly significant effects.

==================================================================
RIGHT TO ERASURE — 'RIGHT TO BE FORGOTTEN' (Article 17)
==================================================================
The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the controller the erasure of
personal data concerning them without undue delay, and the controller shall have the
obligation to erase personal data without undue delay where one of the following grounds applies:

(a) the personal data are no longer necessary for the purposes for which they were
    collected or otherwise processed;
(b) the data subject withdraws consent on which the processing is based, and there is
    no other legal ground for the processing;
(c) the data subject objects to the processing under Article 21 and there are no
    overriding legitimate grounds;
(d) the personal data have been unlawfully processed;
(e) the personal data must be erased to comply with a legal obligation;
(f) the personal data were collected in relation to the offer of information society
    services to a child.

The right to erasure is not absolute. It does not apply to the extent that processing
is necessary for exercising the right of freedom of expression and information, for
compliance with a legal obligation, for reasons of public interest in the area of
public health, for archiving, scientific or historical research purposes, or for the
establishment, exercise, or defence of legal claims.

==================================================================
DATA PROTECTION OFFICER — WHEN REQUIRED (Article 37)
==================================================================
The controller and the processor shall designate a data protection officer (DPO) in
any case where:

(a) the processing is carried out by a public authority or body, except for courts
    acting in their judicial capacity;
(b) the core activities of the controller or processor consist of processing operations
    which, by virtue of their nature, scope, or purposes, require regular and systematic
    monitoring of data subjects on a large scale; or
(c) the core activities consist of processing on a large scale of special categories of
    data (Article 9) or personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences (Article 10).

A group of undertakings may appoint a single DPO provided that the officer is easily
accessible from each establishment. The DPO shall be designated on the basis of
professional qualities and expert knowledge of data protection law and practices, and
may be a staff member or fulfil the tasks under a service contract. The DPO must be
involved in all issues relating to the protection of personal data, report to the
highest management level, and must not be dismissed or penalised for performing their tasks.

==================================================================
PENALTIES AND ADMINISTRATIVE FINES (Article 83)
==================================================================
Administrative fines for infringements shall in each individual case be effective,
proportionate, and dissuasive. GDPR sets two tiers of maximum fines:

Lower tier — up to 10,000,000 EUR, or in the case of an undertaking, up to 2% of the
total worldwide annual turnover of the preceding financial year, whichever is higher.
This tier applies to infringements of obligations such as those relating to controllers
and processors (Articles 8, 11, 25–39, 42, 43), certification bodies, and monitoring bodies.

Higher tier — up to 20,000,000 EUR, or in the case of an undertaking, up to 4% of the
total worldwide annual turnover of the preceding financial year, whichever is higher.
This tier applies to infringements of the basic principles for processing, including
conditions for consent (Articles 5, 6, 7, and 9), the data subjects' rights (Articles
12–22), and transfers of personal data to a recipient in a third country (Articles 44–49).

When deciding the amount, supervisory authorities consider the nature, gravity, and
duration of the infringement, whether it was intentional or negligent, actions taken to
mitigate damage, the degree of cooperation, and the categories of personal data affected.

==================================================================
BREACH NOTIFICATION (Articles 33–34)
==================================================================
In the case of a personal data breach, the controller shall notify the competent
supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours
after having become aware of it, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to
the rights and freedoms of natural persons. When the breach is likely to result in a
high risk, the controller shall also communicate it to the affected data subjects
without undue delay.
